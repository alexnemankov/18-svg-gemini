🏛️ Regulatory Philosophy & Framework
The European Union approaches artificial intelligence as a horizontal product-safety and fundamental rights problem. It establishes a tiered risk classification model with strict prohibitions on unacceptable practices, heavy compliance mandates on high-risk deployments, and systemic risk governance for frontier foundation models.
Full enforcement of High-Risk AI system requirements and General Purpose AI (GPAI) transparency obligations takes effect in 2026, requiring post-market monitoring, cybersecurity testing, and data governance certification.
📜 Key Provisions & Compliance Mandates
  • Categorical ban on biometric social scoring and untargeted facial scraping
  • Mandatory conformity assessments & CE marking for High-Risk AI systems
  • Systemic risk evaluation & red-teaming for GPAI models above 10^25 FLOPs
  • Fines up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover for non-compliance
Strategic Impact: The "Brussels Effect" forces multinational AI developers to align global architecture with EU safety documentation to maintain single-market access.
